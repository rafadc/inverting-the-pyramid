Chapter 18

Total Recall

Amsterdam, 1991. Twenty years after Michels and Cruyff, Louis van Gaal inherits an Ajax with little money and a great deal of memory, and rebuilds Total Football as a system: a back three that marks zonally, a defender who is the playmaker, a diamond in midfield and wingers who are never overlapped. Across the Atlantic, Marcelo Bielsa is rebuilding it as a religion of pressing.

A 4-3-3 that was really a 3-4-3

Van Gaal made explicit what had long been implicit at Ajax: with one central defender stepping out to hold in midfield, the 4-3-3 becomes a 3-4-3. Three defenders marking zonally, a diamond in the middle, two wingers flanking a striker.

When Ajax beat Torino to win the 1992 Uefa Cup the man stepping out was Wim Jonk; Frank de Boer and Danny Blind were the full-backs and only Sonny Silooy was a defender by trade. Here is the 1995 side making the same move.

Ajax

The playmaker wears No. 4

‘In modern football the players in the middle of the back four… have really become playmakers.’ – Louis van Gaal

The No. 10 had become too crowded to control the tempo, so Van Gaal handed it to the man behind the midfield. He brought Frank Rijkaard back from Milan to be his 4; at Euro 96 Guus Hiddink gave the same job to Clarence Seedorf, who was not a defender in any sense. Rijkaard drops between the centre-backs to receive and steps back out to distribute to either flank.

The diamond turns

Giving the playmaking to the 4 changed the 10. Van Gaal used Bergkamp and then Rob Alflen, but his ideal was Jari Litmanen, signed in 1992. The diamond rotates forward with him, and the centre-forward, Stefan Pettersson or Ronald de Boer, is judged on one-twos and the space he creates rather than goals.

‘When Ajax lose possession he immediately carries out his defensive tasks, and when Ajax are in possession he chooses the right moment to appear alongside the centre-forward as the second striker.’ – Kormelink and Seeverens on Litmanen

Wingers keep their space

Here Van Gaal broke with the seventies. Midfielders were not allowed to run beyond their wingers to overlap: a failed overlap exposed the full-back, and the winger always needed space ahead of him to attack. The midfielder stays inside for support and to switch the ball quickly to the other flank. Ajax played, in Van Gaal’s words, ‘in a small area of the field’.

5:3

Van Gaal’s favourite drill, the one he was failed on when he took his coaching badge because it ‘didn’t relate to a real football situation’. Four players work the sides of a rectangle; a fifth stands in the middle, surrounded by three defenders, and may only play one-touch. Possession under pressure, triangles everywhere.

He also played six against seven on half a pitch, the seven without a goalkeeper so that they had to press rapidly or be caught by long shots.

Five Three

Vienna, 24 May 1995: the first half

Fabio Capello’s Milan, 4–0 winners over Cruyff’s Barcelona in the previous final, played their 4-4-2 with Boban ahead of Desailly and two traps. Massaro pulled right whenever Blind or Rijkaard had the ball to cut off the pass to Frank de Boer, the better long passer; Boban sat on Rijkaard. So Ajax built through Reiziger on the right, whose long balls to Ronald de Boer went astray, Desailly smothered Litmanen, and Blind was left hitting it long to Finidi George again and again.

Ajax Milan

Half-time: Rijkaard drops

Van Gaal told Rijkaard to drop deeper, away from Boban, and play more as part of a back four: time and space to dictate. Eight minutes into the half Kanu replaced Seedorf and Frank de Boer moved into right midfield, pushing higher than Seedorf had and driving Albertini back. Then Kluivert came on for Litmanen, Kanu became the 10 and began to get the better of a weary Desailly. Ajax had 60 per cent of the second-half possession.

Ajax Milan

Eighty-fifth minute

Overmars started the move down the left, Kanu found the pass, Kluivert scored. 1–0. A young side on a small budget had out-passed the champions of Europe: the vindication of Van Gaal’s philosophy. They reached the final again in 1996 and lost to Juventus on penalties; then Bosman and the distribution of Champions League money broke the side up, and Van Gaal followed Cruyff’s path to Barcelona, where he won two titles while the pair squabbled ‘like two Marxist theorists debating obscure doctrinal details’.

Lusail, 2022

Van Gaal was never quite the same either. The Netherlands job, a second Barça spell and a stint as Ajax sporting director all failed; AZ, champions in 2009 with a more attacking style, Bayern, where he laid the foundations for Heynckes and Guardiola, and a counter-attacking Netherlands at the 2014 World Cup went better. At Manchester United he tried to reinstate the true Ajax principles and got possession far from goal, going nowhere.

By the 2022 World Cup quarter-final against Argentina he had made his peace with the direct game: Luuk de Jong, Wout Weghorst and briefly Virgil van Dijk went up front and the Dutch smacked it long to them, forcing extra time. The canal-belt intellectuals were as outraged as the Van Gaal of twenty years earlier would have been.

Rosario: the other Total Football

Where Van Gaal prioritised possession, Marcelo Bielsa prioritised pressing. A failed Newell’s Old Boys player who carried a thesaurus onto the training pitch, indexed his copies of El Gráfico, watched thousands of videos and took fifty-one of them to his Vélez interview, he looked for a third way between menottismo and bilardismo. Defending was proactivity: win the ball back as close to the opponent’s goal as possible, with a team never more than 25 metres from front to back.

‘My football, in defence, is very simple: “We run all the time.”’ – Marcelo Bielsa
Newell’s Old Boys Opponents

Hotel Conquistador, February 1992

After a 6–0 home defeat to San Lorenzo in the Libertadores, Bielsa shut himself in a darkened hotel room in Santa Fe and wept. His conclusion was not that he had gone too far but that he had not gone far enough. Saldaña went from right-back to left; Berizzo from the left of defence to the back of midfield; Gamboa, a natural libero, to the right with a brief to tuck in behind Pochettino; Llop from full-back into the centre. Newell’s lost once in winning the clausura and reached the Libertadores final.

Newell’s Old Boys

Polifuncionalidad

The 3-4-3 was the basic shape but, as Sacchi had argued, positions have value only relative to other positions. If Gerardo Martino found two holding players in front of him, Alfredo Berti pushed up so he did not face ‘an unequal struggle’. That left Berizzo alone against two creators, so either Llop stepped up into Berti’s place, or Saldaña stepped into Berizzo’s and Berizzo ran to Berti’s original spot. Both were valid; the players chose.

‘Concentración permanente, movilidad, rotación y repentización.’
Newell’s Old Boys Opponents

A line 140 years long

Bielsa has won little, three Argentinian titles, an Olympic gold and a promotion, and his sides burn out, as Athletic did in the spring of 2012. But Sampaoli, Pochettino, Martino and Simeone all passed through his crucible, and the most successful bielsista of all is Pep Guardiola, who took over the Bayern that Van Gaal had built. From the 2-3-5 of the 1880s through Michels, Van Gaal and Bielsa to Guardiola, it is one philosophical line.
